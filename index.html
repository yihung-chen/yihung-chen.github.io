Thank you for visiting my website.

I am a Ph.D. student in Political Science at National Taiwan University (NTU). I hold an M.A. in Political Science from NTU and a B.A. in Political Economy from National Sun Yat-sen University.

My primary research interests include electoral politics, political parties, political behavior, and political economy, with a regional focus on Taiwan. My research examines how political parties shape the career paths and electoral strategies of political elites, how coordination among political actors influences electoral outcomes, and how foreign economic pressure and political information shape public opinion and political behavior.

My current work explores several related questions: how intra-party nomination rules constrain political ambition and shape accountability; how lower-level political elites influence higher-level electoral outcomes; how foreign economic coercion affects electoral behavior; and how political conflict and economic pressure shape public attitudes toward vaccines and international partners. Methodologically, I use survey experiments, quasi-experimental designs, and causal inference methods for observational data.
